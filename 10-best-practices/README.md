## Key takeaways

- Keyboard shortcuts (`c`, `/`, `.`, `s` then `t`, `s` then `b`, and `g` then `i`) reduce mouse travel for create, search, actions, and backlog reordering; press `?` to see the current list on your site.
- Bulk selection, right-click moves, and list view make multi-item edits practical for backlogs and release sweeps.
- Good etiquette means short summaries, rich descriptions, honest statuses, comments with @mentions, and updating only work you own.
- Labels, components, and fix versions only help when spelling and usage stay consistent; select existing labels instead of retyping them.
- Missing board cards are usually filtered out: check quick filters, status-to-column mapping, board filter JQL, and on Kanban boards the sub-filter and timeframe.
- The final challenge repeats the full user workflow in a company-managed Scrum sandbox — varied work types, organizing fields, cross-space filters, and quick filters on a theme you choose.
- Lasting skill comes from small real-world improvements and regular practice, then optional depth in administration and Agile frameworks.

## Hands-on practice

Suggested exercises for a free Jira Cloud site (these are not part of the official course material):

1. Press `?` and practise `c`, `/`, `s` then `t`, and `s` then `b` on a backlog until you can create and reorder work items without the mouse.
2. Open list view, add three columns, and inline-edit five work items in one sitting.
3. On a board, hide a work item on purpose — epic quick filter, unmapped status, or a restrictive board filter clause — then walk the troubleshooting checklist until it reappears.
4. Complete the [final challenge](04-final-challenge.md) using one of the suggested themes (cupcake delivery, film night, or world tour) with at least two quick filters and one fix version.
5. Draft a team etiquette one-pager from the do's and don'ts table: summary rules, label naming, and when to comment versus reassign.
