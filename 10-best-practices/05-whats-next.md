# What's Next on Your Jira Journey: Advanced Skills and Resources

[Course home](../README.md) › [Section 10: Best Practices and Final Challenge](README.md) › Lesson 5 · 3:20

## TL;DR

The course covered everyday Jira use from spaces and boards through JQL, dashboards, reports, and lightweight automation. The next step is to apply small improvements where you already work — or keep a personal sandbox alive — then deepen administration skills and Agile practice when you are ready. Value comes from using Jira correctly over time, not from collecting features you never touch.

## Key concepts

- **Incremental adoption**: Small, consistent changes (shared labels, clearer components, one useful dashboard) often beat a big-bang reconfiguration.
- **Personal sandbox**: A free or private space where you can rehearse new techniques before suggesting them to a team.
- **Administration depth**: Space schemes, permissions, workflow design, and marketplace apps sit outside this user-focused course but matter when you configure Jira for others.
- **Agile literacy**: Boards and sprints in Jira make more sense when you also understand Scrum, Kanban, and team facilitation — complementary skills, not substitutes.

## Next steps

## How to do it in Jira

### Apply one improvement at work

1. Observe one pain point (duplicate labels, invisible work, noisy email, manual handoffs).
2. Pick the smallest Jira change that addresses it — an agreed label list, a quick filter, a checklist template, or one automation rule.
3. Demo the change to two teammates, gather feedback, and iterate.

### Keep skills fresh in a sandbox

1. Maintain the space from the final challenge or create a new one on [Jira Cloud Free](../01-introduction/04-set-up-free-jira-cloud-site.md).
2. Each month, add one feature you have not used recently — a new gadget, a JQL filter, or a Kanban WIP limit.
3. When Jira updates its interface, press `?` and skim release notes so shortcuts and menus stay familiar.

### Plan deeper learning

1. List admin tasks your team currently waits on (new workflows, custom fields, scheme changes).
2. Read Atlassian documentation for those areas or follow a dedicated administration course.
3. Pair with your organization's Jira admin on a low-risk change before editing production schemes alone.

## Examples

A team already tracks bugs in Jira but never uses components. You propose three components (*Web*, *Mobile*, *API*), re-tag open bugs in a lunch session, and add a component quick filter to the board. No workflow change required — visibility improves immediately.

A learner without a workplace Jira site runs a *World tour planner* space from the final challenge, then migrates their weekly learning goals into the same backlog so shortcuts and JQL stay muscle memory.

## Tips and pitfalls

- **Any improvement counts.** Cleaner summaries, consistent labels, or one shared dashboard saves more time than a perfect process nobody follows.
- **Tool skill is not team process.** Jira proficiency works best paired with agreed Agile ceremonies and clear ownership on the team.

> [!NOTE]
> Atlassian continues renaming interface elements and shipping experiments. Re-read [Jira terminology changes](../reference/jira-terminology-changes.md) when documentation and the UI disagree.
